Own a share of the machines.

AI is becoming the engine of the economy, and whoever owns that engine will collect its income. Evolve_X is a blueprint for a cooperative that lets ordinary people own it together: member-owned computing, AI that works for you instead of on you, and a share of the surplus that grows as the network grows.

Planned to start in Dallas–Fort Worth and Amarillo. Designed so any town can open the next chapter.

The shift

In 2026 the largest technology companies planned to spend more than $700 billion on AI infrastructure in a single year.1

That capacity is starting to do a growing share of the world’s work: answering calls, drafting contracts, reading scans, writing code. The work doesn’t disappear. Its income moves to whoever owns the machines. The question that will decide the next fifty years is simple: who owns them, and where does their income go?

Two futures

The same machines can lead to either one. The difference is who owns them.

Rent the future

A few companies own the machines. Everyone else rents access, gives away their data, and waits for a check. If a universal income arrives, it arrives on someone else’s terms and can be taken back the same way.

Own the future

People hold a real stake in the machines that replace and amplify their work. Income arrives as savings, wages, dividends and, in time, a public share of the AI economy. Nobody has to wait for permission to start.

Evolve_X is a practical way to build the second future, starting now, under laws that already exist.

How it works

A cycle: each turn makes the next one larger.

  1. Join with one member share. You get one vote, access to the co-op’s services, and your own ownership account.
  2. The co-op owns its computing and builds AI services. A personal advocate for members, and practical tools for local businesses.
  3. Businesses and members pay for those services. Local hubs also earn from the power grid, training contracts and spare capacity.
  4. The surplus comes back. After fair wages and reserves, what’s left returns to members by how much they used the co-op: part in cash, part into ownership.
  5. More members bring more capacity. Better services and larger dividends follow, and each chapter that succeeds helps open the next.

Eight pillars

Each is a service worth having on its own. Ownership is what makes it fair. Every one has its own seal, because every one is a share of the same commons.

  • OwnMember-owned computing and local businesses, so AI’s income stays with the people who use it.
  • WorkLiving wages, a published pay ratio, and paid apprenticeships into AI-era jobs.
  • ShareSpending at member businesses builds your ownership instead of vanishing into card fees.
  • ProtectAn AI advocate that fights denied claims, junk fees and billing errors on your side.
  • SettleTaxes set aside automatically, and every agreement written in plain English.
  • CreateArtists and writers get credit and payment when AI learns from their work.
  • LearnEducator-owned lessons and AI skills for every age, tied to real jobs.
  • HomeHelp for families facing foreclosure to keep their equity and their homes.

Different by design

  • No coin to speculate on. Membership is for using and governing the co-op, not for trading.
  • No promised returns. Dividends depend on real surplus, and the math is public.
  • One member, one vote. Money never buys control.
  • Open books. Finances, the pay ratio and the rules behind every automated decision are open to members.
  • A locked mission. An independent trustee can block any attempt to sell the co-op or change its purpose.
  • A person to appeal to. Every automated decision that affects a member can be taken to a human.

Starting in Texas

Room to build, a grid that pays flexible users to ease off at peak times, millions of small businesses, and a long habit of public ownership.

Texas has paid for its schools with oil royalties for generations. The first chapter is planned for Dallas–Fort Worth, with Amarillo close behind, then Austin, Houston and San Antonio. Every chapter after that runs on the same open playbook.
