How the money works

A cooperative is simple. It sells useful things, pays its people fairly, saves for hard times, and returns what’s left to the members who used it. Here is how Evolve_X would earn, spend and share, with a calculator so you can test every assumption yourself.

Where the money comes from

Where it goes

In this order, every year.

  1. Fair wages first. Every worker earns at least a living wage, and the highest pay in the co-op is capped at five times the lowest.
  2. Running costs. Computing, power, buildings, software, and dues that fund the federation’s shared services.
  3. Reserves and growth. A share of each year’s surplus is saved for hard times and invested in the next hub.
  4. Patronage. Everything left goes back to members in proportion to what they spent with the co-op.

Your ownership account

American cooperatives can return surplus to members as patronage dividends. Under federal tax rules, at least 20 percent of a qualified allocation has to be paid in cash. The rest can be credited to your ownership account, where it funds the co-op’s growth and is paid back to you on a published schedule, usually after several years.

That is how a member’s stake builds year after year without anyone buying or selling a speculative token. Each member holds one member share, which carries one vote. A bigger ownership account never buys more votes.

Why the machines alone won’t make you rich

A tempting idea in this space is that a computer in your home can pay for itself by renting out its processing power. For most people the honest math says otherwise. Renting out computing is a commodity business where the largest data centers win on scale, and a home machine’s earnings are usually modest once the electricity bill is counted.

The income is in what the machines do: services people need, built and owned by the people who need them. That is why Evolve_X sells services, not hardware, and why it will never sell a device on the promise of income.

What we will never sell

  • Coins or tokens marketed as investments.
  • Hardware sold with income projections.
  • Lease-back or “passive return” programs.
  • A premium on your purchases in exchange for a future payout.
  • Your data.

If the co-op ever raises money from the public, it will do so through a properly registered or legally exempt offering, with full disclosure of the risks.
