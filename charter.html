The charter

Institutions drift. They start with a purpose and slowly bend toward whatever is convenient for the people running them. This charter is built to resist that in its structure, not only in its words.

Articles

Draft for public comment. The founding members will adopt the final text.

  1. Open membership. Anyone can join, whatever their faith, politics, origin, income or record.
  2. One member, one vote. Capital can earn a limited return. It never buys control.
  3. Surplus returns to the people who use the co-op. After fair wages and reserves, surplus goes back to members in proportion to their use.
  4. Open books. Finances, pay, major contracts and the rules of every automated decision are published to members.
  5. Plain language. If members can’t understand an agreement, the co-op can’t use it.
  6. Privacy by design. Collect the least, keep it on the member’s side wherever possible, and never sell it.
  7. A human to appeal to. Every automated decision that affects a member can be appealed to a person.
  8. A locked mission. The purpose can’t be changed, and the co-op can’t be sold or converted to a for-profit company, without a supermajority of members and the consent of the mission trustee.
  9. Cooperation. Work alongside other co-ops, credit unions, unions and public institutions rather than trying to replace them.
  10. Dignity for everyone. The co-op honors people of every faith, nation and background, and takes no part in partisan campaigns.

Built against drift

Drawn from research on infrastructure drift and immutable intent.

  • A purpose test. Before launch, every new product must show in writing how it raises members’ monthly surplus.
  • An annual drift audit. An outside reviewer compares what the co-op actually does with what this charter says, and the gap is published.
  • Sunset by default. New programs expire after three years unless members vote to renew them.
  • Easy exit. Members can leave and receive their ownership account on the published schedule. Their data leaves with them.
  • No single leader. Nobody may hold more than one of these roles at once: board chair, chief executive, mission trustee.

Lines we won’t cross

  • We won’t sell tokens, coins or devices as investments, or promise returns.
  • We won’t use names, seals or language that suggest we are, or are endorsed by, a government agency, central bank or court.
  • We won’t claim a partnership, endorsement or press coverage we can’t document.
  • We won’t build biometric databases or sell member data.
  • We won’t endorse candidates or parties.
  • We won’t publish accusations against individuals. Disputes belong with courts and regulators.
  • We won’t spread conspiracy theories or demean any people, faith or nation.

AI accountability

  • Plain descriptions. Every AI system members use comes with a plain description of what it does, what it was trained on and where it fails.
  • Incident reports. Harmful errors and data exposures are reported to members within 72 hours.
  • People in the loop. A trained person reviews any decision about a member’s money, health, housing or work.
  • Your file, your view. Members can see and correct what the co-op’s AI knows about them.

Critique welcome

Good ideas survive scrutiny. Anyone can file a critique of this blueprint in public, and every serious critique gets a public answer.
