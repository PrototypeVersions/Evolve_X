The big picture

AI will make the world far more productive. Whether that becomes broad prosperity or deeper concentration depends on choices about ownership being made right now.

Where AI is taking the economy

  • Work is moving to machines. AI agents already draft, review, answer, schedule and code. The tasks don’t vanish; their income moves to whoever owns the capacity that performs them.
  • That capacity is concentrating. Frontier AI needs data centers, chips and electricity on a scale only a handful of companies can fund, and they planned more than $700 billion of it for 2026 alone.1
  • Services are getting cheap. A legal letter, a tax return or an insurance appeal now costs a fraction of what it did. That is a gift to households, but only if the savings reach them instead of becoming another platform’s margin.
  • Trust is scarce. People are being asked to hand their data, identity and judgement to systems they can’t see into and have no say over.

Who gets the income?

Three serious answers are on the table. Each solves part of the problem.

AnswerWhat it meansStrengthLimit
Universal high income Governments send generous checks, paid for by the abundance AI and robots create. Elon Musk proposed it in April 2026.2 Reaches everyone. Depends on politics and on taxing the companies that control the machines. Recipients have no say in how the machines are run.
Universal basic compute Everyone gets a slice of AI capacity to use, sell or donate. Sam Altman floated it in 2024.3 Gives people a productive tool rather than only cash. The slice is defined, priced and run by whoever owns the model.
Universal ownership People hold real equity and votes in productive enterprises: cooperatives, employee ownership and public funds. Alaska’s oil fund paid every eligible resident $1,000 in 2025.4 Income plus control, compounding over time. Has to be built, one enterprise and one fund at a time.

A good future needs all three. Checks are the floor, compute is the tool, and ownership is what keeps the floor rising and the tools honest. A cooperative can build the ownership leg today, without waiting for Washington, and it can be a credible voice for the public leg.

The ladder to high income

From what helps this month to what compounds over a decade.

  1. Spend less. An advocate that catches billing errors, finds benefits you qualify for, and appeals denials you would have accepted.
  2. Get some back. Patronage: part of what you spend with the co-op and its member businesses returns to you.
  3. Earn more. Good jobs and paid apprenticeships inside the network, at a living wage or better.
  4. Own more. Your ownership account grows each year and pays out on a published schedule.
  5. Share the commons. A public AI dividend, funded by the AI economy itself and paid equally to everyone.

No single rung is a high income. Together, compounding for a decade across millions of members, they can be.

Centralize or decentralize?

Federated: decide locally unless a decision clearly works better at scale, and never put a decision where members can’t see it.

Both sides of this argument are half right. Centralized systems are efficient, but they concentrate power and drift toward serving themselves. Fully decentralized systems resist capture, but they are often slow, unaccountable and easy to game. Evolve_X is federated: ownership and decisions sit as close to people as possible, and only what genuinely benefits from scale is shared.

Surplus, not debt

Millions of households are one unexpected bill away from a crisis, however strong the headline numbers look. So Evolve_X measures itself by member surplus: what a typical member has left at the end of the month after the essentials are paid.

Every product has to answer one question before it launches: does it raise the typical member’s monthly surplus? If the answer is no, it doesn’t ship.

A public dividend for the AI age

Texas already knows how to do this. It has paid for its public schools with oil royalties for generations, and Alaska pays every resident a yearly dividend from its oil fund. AI is built on public goods too: public research, the public grid, and the writing, music and art of billions of people.

Evolve_X will advocate for an AI Commons Fund: a permanent public fund that collects a small royalty on large-scale AI computing and pays an equal dividend to every resident. The cooperative builds ownership from the bottom up. The fund builds it from the top down. Each makes the other more likely.

Foundations

This blueprint builds on research by Dr. Blake Austin Myers on two questions. The first is wage integrity: why steady, full-time work no longer guarantees a livable life. The second is infrastructure drift: how systems slowly forget their purpose unless that purpose is built into their structure. The first explains why the economy needs to change. The second shapes how Evolve_X is governed, so it can’t become what it set out to replace.
