Hard questions

If a plan can’t answer the uncomfortable questions plainly, it isn’t ready. Here are the ones people ask first.

Money

Is joining an investment?

No. A member share gives you one vote and access to the co-op’s services. Patronage depends on real surplus and could be small, or nothing, in a hard year. Nobody should join expecting a financial return.

Is this crypto?

No. Evolve_X has no coin or token. Money moves in dollars over ordinary bank payment rails, and ownership is recorded the way cooperatives have always recorded it.

Do I need to buy a computer or a device?

No. Services work on the phone or computer you already have. The co-op’s machines live in its hubs, and it will never sell you a device on the promise that it will earn money.

What will it cost to join?

The plan is a one-time member share of around $100, with waivers for people who can’t afford it, plus an optional monthly plan for personal services. The founding board will set final prices, and members can change them.

Why not just wait for a universal income?

We hope a strong public floor arrives, and we’ll push for one. But a check that depends on whoever controls the machines is fragile. Ownership is income you have a vote over, and you can start building it now.

Trust

How is this different from a credit union or an ordinary co-op?

The principles are the same. The tools are new. Evolve_X applies cooperative ownership to AI and computing, links chapters into a federation so they can share costly things, and locks its mission so it can’t be bought out.

What stops it from turning into the thing it set out to replace?

Structure, not good intentions: one member one vote, worker seats on every board, a mission trustee who can block a sale or a change of purpose, term limits, open books, and a public drift audit every year. The charter has the details.

Who is behind this?

Right now Evolve_X is an open blueprint published for comment. The cooperative itself will be formed with a founding board and governed by its members from the start.

What happens to my data?

The co-op collects as little as possible, keeps it under your control where it can, and never sells it. You can see it, correct it, and take it with you when you leave.

What if the AI gets something wrong?

A trained person reviews anything that affects your money, health, housing or work, every automated decision can be appealed to a person, and serious errors are reported to members within 72 hours.

People

Can someone with a criminal record join or work here?

Yes. A record doesn’t bar membership, and it only matters for a job where the law requires it.

Does the co-op take political or religious sides?

No. People of every faith and none, every party and none, are equally welcome. The co-op advocates for policies that broaden ownership, such as a public AI dividend, but it never endorses candidates.

I don’t live in Texas. Can I take part?

Not as a chapter member yet, but you can help shape the blueprint now, and the chapter playbook is being written so any town can open one.

How can I help right now?

Read the blueprint, try to break it, and post your critique on GitHub. If you run a business, teach, make art, or work in insurance, housing, law or computing, your experience is exactly what the next version needs.
